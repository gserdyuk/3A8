# Status — 2026-08-27 — what the chaotic movement turned into, and the goal it is now held to

**Purpose.** How the project is seen today, written after an audit of the whole course of work and
after an external human scale for estimation was found and read. It supersedes `docs/status_2026-08-25.md`
where the two disagree, and it replaces `docs/exit_criterion.md` v1.0 as the definition of done —
v2.0 is stated in §6 below and the old file carries a pointer to it.

Facts (§1–§3) are separated from readings (§4–§5) and from the goal (§6–§7), per house discipline.

**What is new since 25 August, and it is the reason this file exists:** an external measurement of how
much *human professional estimators* disagree with each other on one and the same requirement
specification. The project had never had that number. Having it changes what the instrument should be
measured against, and therefore what the project is for.

---

## 1. The course of work, in four movements

**1. The start — both halves judged by a model.** Bottom-up WBS plus a reference class, each produced
by asking a model for both the *structure* of the work and the *magnitude* of each piece.

**2. The variance, which did not yield.** Repeats of one pinned prompt disagreed by ×1.3–2 on the
total. Five versions of form-constraining rules — leaf size, coverage at every split, item provenance
— none narrowed the spread. `run14` closed the question by measuring the floor: a batch with **no
method at all** gave CV 8.55%, and no version of the method ever significantly beat it. All ten
no-method runs, unprompted, produced the same shape — decomposition into 10–19 blocks, a role uplift,
a contingency percentage. The general finding:

> Rules that constrain the *form* of an estimate bind exactly what they name, and the magnitude
> relocates into whichever variable was left free.

**3. The one architectural move — the magnitude leaves the model.** *Numbers come from a table; the
model only chooses rows.* Three sensors that produce no effort figures of any kind, one pinned table
of constants they never see, and a script that joins them. Measured effect on repeat-run disagreement
of the total: **×1.288 → ×1.032**, on two cases.

**4. Three sanitary episodes, each unglamorous and each load-bearing.**
- **Units** (three days). One declared unit, the rate table in net person-hours, a required unit
  declaration from every role that produces a number. This alone moved FaxRxTx from ×1.735 to **×1.21**.
- **Constants** (one ruling). Six numbers about time were in circulation; five described how an
  organisation behaves, not how work behaves, and left the method. **Four constants remain** in the method itself; a fifth, the presence conversion, was pinned on
  2026-08-27 in the **comparison layer** — §4a of that file — because leaving it to the reader meant
  re-arguing it after every result.
- **Isolation.** Gap-blind sensors, a sealed `FACT.md`, md5-pinned inputs, verbatim transcripts.
  Eleven of eleven sensors caught and quarantined an injected input on 2026-08-22.

## 2. What exists today

| step | role | what it does | measured repeatability |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | orchestrator | pin the requirement list + md5, split, assumption log, technology declaration | a human act |
| 1 | `Hotyn-M 1.1` | product model from requirements | **Jaccard 0.31 (BMS) / 0.41 (FaxRxTx); structure size ×1.56 (BMS) / ×1.02 (FaxRxTx)** |
| 2 | `Hotyn-W 1.1` | cross with the technology declaration → work model | Jaccard **0.969** — very nearly a function |
| 3 | `Hotyn-D 2.0` | size classes, no numbers | 87.3% on classes → **×1.03** on the priced total |
| 4 | a script | arithmetic against the pinned rate table | exact |
| 5 | `Lytin-R 1.0` | the outside view, a named project class | P50s **×1.31** apart |
| 6 | `Lytin-K 1.0` + `Lytin-G 1.0` | diagnose divergence, transfer parameters, final range | has run **once** in this generation |

**The single most useful thing this table says:** the variance did not disappear — it **concentrated
into step 1**. Steps 2–4 are practically deterministic. The project went from "the whole thing is
noisy" to "one named step is noisy", which is the difference between a mess and an engineering problem.

**Score on the only case with an outcome.** FaxRxTx: chain **99.4 staffed person-months** against a
fact of **120** — ×1.21 low, both classification repeats inside the gate, nothing fitted on the case.

## 3. The external human scale — new, and it reframes everything

**Jørgensen & Grimstad, Simula Research Laboratory, IEEE TSE 2011.** 240 companies invited, **46 paid
to estimate the same five requirement specifications** — Russia 15, Ukraine 5, India 7, Pakistan 5,
Bulgaria 4, Romania 3, Belarus 2, and five more countries. Companies from under 10 to over 1000
employees. **They were not told it was research**; it was ordinary paid estimation work. Each was
screened for relevant professional experience by CV — an ordinary competent market sample, not a
curated elite panel. Each delivered not only a number but the proposed architecture, the development
platform, the **technology choices**, the assumptions, a work breakdown, an uncertainty assessment and
a description of its own estimation process.

Table 3 of that paper, effort estimates in work-hours:

| spec | domain | pages | min | Q1 | median | Q3 | max | Q3/Q1 | max/min |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| S1 | dinner reservation | 5 | 45 | 132 | 193 | 339 | 1320 | ×2.6 | ×29 |
| S2 | doctor's appointments | 12 | 61 | 186 | 330 | 438 | 1200 | ×2.4 | ×20 |
| S3 | organisation info site | 4 | 160 | 316 | 545 | 729 | 2280 | ×2.3 | ×14 |
| S4 | inventory and work orders | 19 | 240 | 681 | 895 | 1316 | 3371 | ×1.9 | ×14 |
| S5 | research database + site | 11 | 18 | 134 | 190 | 347 | 1160 | ×2.6 | ×64 |

**And two anchors to fact.** S3's real implementation took approximately **800 work-hours** against a
no-anchor median estimate of 568 — the market median missed **×1.41 low**. S5 was implemented by four
different companies at **300–900 work-hours** against a median estimate of 190.

**Declared limitation on transferring this scale.** These are 45–3371 work-hour projects, small web
applications. Our two cases are 10 000–20 000 hours. The scale is real and it is the only one we have;
it was not taken at our size, and every use of it carries that caveat.

## 4. Readings — what any of this may claim

**4a. What is genuinely established** is not an accuracy, it is a construction principle:

> **The magnitude must live in a table, not in the model.** Measured on two cases, against a measured
> no-method floor, with the mechanism named.

That is a transferable statement about how to build any LLM-based estimation instrument, and it is
worth more than any single number this project has produced.

**4b. Against the human scale, the instrument looks good on both axes the goal names:**

| | market, 46 companies | this instrument |
|---|---|---|
| disagreement between "estimators" on one text | interquartile **×1.9–2.6**, full range ×14–64 | ×1.03 on classification — **end to end not measured** |
| miss of the median against fact | **×1.41–2.4 low** | ×1.21 low, n = 1 |

n is tiny on our side. The direction is not ambiguous.

**4c. What this says about the old gate.** `exit_criterion.md` v1.0 demanded the calibrated P50 land
within **×1.3** of the fact. The interquartile spread of professional companies estimating one and the
same specification is ×1.9–2.6. **The old gate demanded that the instrument be roughly twice as
accurate as half the professional market can agree with itself.** That is not a strict standard; it is
a standard from a different world, and it is replaced in §6.

**4d. `L-1` deserves a second look.** The ×1.735 correction fitted on FaxRxTx sits suspiciously close
to how much the market median misses low on S3 (×1.41) and S5. The open question is no longer "is
`L-1` legitimate" but **"is `L-1` a correction for our method at all, or a correction for the
corpus-average optimism any such tool inherits?"**

## 5. What is missing — stated without softening

- ~~**The chain's end-to-end repeatability has never been measured.**~~ **Measured 2026-08-27
  (run 42): ×1.0532.** `HM29-OA2` was crossed, sized and priced with not one rate changed, giving
  1351.3 table pd against `HM29-OA1`'s centre of 1423.2. **Gate v2.0 test 1 passes with a five-fold
  margin**, and the figure is 3.0× tighter than the no-method baseline's own pairwise agreement on the
  same input — **the first evidence the project has ever had that the apparatus buys something
  measurable over the bare corpus.** A quarter of the gap is the model-bracket step, which is pure
  arithmetic. `examples/FaxRxTx/run42_measurement.md`.
  **Carried, and it is not a formality:** this was measured on the case whose two models agree best
  (×1.024 on the anchored part). **The BMS pair differs by ×1.56 in structure size and has not been
  measured end to end** — `BACKLOG.md` item 1a.
- **No corridor.** One of the gate's three tests cannot be scored at all.
- **No calibration against any outcome.** The rates are external norms fixed by decision.
- **No brake on over-counting.** The construction only adds: accretion adds, crossing multiplies,
  splitting inflates, the floor rounds up, nothing may be removed. The reverse audit is designed and
  has never been run. FaxRxTx nevertheless came out *under* the fact, which is unexplained.
- **Speed has never been measured**, though it is part of the product claim.
- ~~**The no-method baseline has never been run on a case with an outcome.**~~ **Run 2026-08-27
  (run 41), and the answer is that accuracy is undecidable at n=1:** in one shared unit the baseline
  is **20 244 net task hours** against the chain's **11 386**. The fact is presence (2 291 present
  days), so the comparison needs a day-yield constant — and that constant decides it. At this
  project's declared **5–6 net h/day the chain is closer** (×1.21 low vs the baseline's ×1.47 high,
  and ×0.99 at the band's bottom); at the ≈7 the baseline runs assumed for themselves, the baseline
  is closer; the crossover is 6.63. **Settled the same day by the author's ruling:** the conversion is
  pinned at 6 net task hours per present day, ×1.10 leave, 21 days a month (`docs/constants.md` §4a),
  now and later, in place of re-arguing an organisation's behaviour every time. Under it the **chain
  is the closer of the two** — ×1.21 low against the baseline's ×1.47 high. Not a victory: n = 1, and
  the fact's own ±20% band still contains the chain. The apparatus is not shown to buy accuracy; it is
  no longer shown to lose to a bare prompt either. The baseline's end-to-end
  spread is CV 13.75%, max/min 1.722 over n=10 - the project's only whole-pipeline repeatability
  measurement, and it belongs to the instrument with no method in it.
  `examples/FaxRxTx/run41_baseline_no_method.md`.

## 6. The goal, and the boundaries — approved 2026-08-27

### 6a. The goal, in one paragraph

> **An instrument that turns RFP-maturity requirement text into an effort estimate in minutes; whose
> run-to-run spread is tighter than professional estimators disagree with each other; whose number
> lands inside the market's interquartile corridor; and which is correctable by a single constant as
> the organisation accumulates its own outcomes.**

The judgement is drawn from the corpus and **does not claim to be better than the market average**.
The product is speed, repeatability and correctability — not insight. Every report states this ceiling
explicitly, the same way it states its unit.

**Why the ceiling is stated rather than hidden.** There is no mechanism to select for expertise inside
a corpus: prompting for a senior estimator selects text that *sounds* like one. Claiming an expert
level would promise a selection the instrument does not perform.

**A consequence that is not optional.** The market median itself misses low by ×1.41–2.4 (§3).
Reproducing the average estimator therefore means reproducing a systematic underestimate.
**Calibration is not an enhancement to this goal; it is the half of it that makes the other half
usable.**

### 6b. Out of scope, declared

| out | why |
|---|---|
| **the parametric FP instrument** | **frozen.** Measured ×22 miss on the one case with a fact; mechanism understood — roughly half the obligations lie outside FP's definition of size, and that is where the difficulty lives (runs 39–40) |
| being more accurate than the market by quality of judgement | no mechanism selects for expertise |
| a single number for a contract without a corridor | `METHODOLOGY.md` §4 has excluded this from day one |
| throughput Monte Carlo, ML regression | no sprint history, no training sample |
| systems outside custom business/web development | both the corpus and the Jørgensen scale were taken there |

### 6c. Exit criterion v2.0 — replaces v1.0

Three tests. **Two of the three need no documented outcome at all**, which is what makes this gate
reachable where v1.0 was not.

1. **Repeatability.** The chain's **end-to-end** spread — from step 1, not from a pinned product model
   — is **no worse than ×1.3**, i.e. materially tighter than the market's interquartile ×1.9–2.6.
   *Needs no fact.* **Measured 2026-08-27 on FaxRxTx: ×1.0532 — passes.** Outstanding on BMS, whose
   model pair is far less stable; one case is not the test.
2. **Position.** The number lands inside the interquartile corridor of ordinary professional
   estimators on the same text. *Needs no fact; needs a human scale on a shared input.*
3. **Calibratability.** One affine correction fitted on cases A and B reduces the miss on a held-out
   case C. *Needs 3 outcomes, not 4+, and it tests transfer rather than raw accuracy.*

**Provenance, carried unchanged from v1.0:** no parameter may be fitted on the case it is scored
against.

**Standing, 2026-08-27 evening:** **test 1 passes on FaxRxTx (×1.0532) and is unmeasured on BMS**;
test 2 has a scale (§3) but no shared input yet; test 3 has 1 of 3 outcomes.
