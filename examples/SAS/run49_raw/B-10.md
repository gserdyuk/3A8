## Reasoning

**What the thing is.** A full-lifecycle build of a three-module B2B platform (Product/GIN, Location/LN, Access Data) on a shared architecture, plus a substantial cross-module platform layer, plus migration off several existing applications, plus a real non-functional programme. Not a greenfield CRUD app: the client explicitly defers the X-Customer Standards and business rules to the project itself ("which will be defined as part of the project"), the identity solution is unfinalised, and the technology platform is unnamed (NFR-1). Those three open ends are where the variance lives, not in the screen count.

**Two routes, then reconciled.**

*Route 1 — size then rate.* Counting named things: ~18 logical data stores (company/prefix, user/role, GIN record, LN record, two hierarchy kinds, publish/subscribe grant, image, notification + preference, help content, report definition, run history, audit trail, industry configuration, sheet template, status/reuse rules, subscription/controlled group) and ~5 external stores (IdM claims, 3scale analytics, SMTP, SMS gateway, legacy sources). Transactions across the three modules and the platform come to roughly 70 inputs, 40 outputs, 35 enquiries. That lands near 850–950 unadjusted function points; the NFR load (NFR-2 volumes, NFR-8 HA, NFR-14 response targets, NFR-21 accessibility, NFR-20 audit-everything, four environments, API parity for every Access Data function) pushes the adjusted size to ~1,000–1,100. At 20–26 hours per point — the band appropriate to an enterprise-grade build with migration, performance engineering and a team new to the domain (A3) — that is **120–170 pm**.

*Reconciliation.* Route 2 is the usual bottom-up overshoot — per-workstream figures each carry their own slack and the slack compounds. Route 1 is the tighter of the two and is anchored to counted artefacts.
