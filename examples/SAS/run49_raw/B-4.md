## Reasoning

**Shape of the thing.** Three modules over one data and service architecture: Product (GIN), Location (LN), Access Data — plus a large cross-cutting layer that the General Requirements and NFRs describe and that neither module owns. Roughly 130 leaf functional statements and 22 NFRs. The heavy items are not the CRUD: they are the workflow engine, the import/export adapter set, the publish/subscribe mechanism, the reporting subsystem over a separate read store, the member-facing API, migration, and the performance targets.

**Method.** Bottom-up by component, each figure already loaded with analysis, design, build, test and defect-fix for that component; management added as a percentage; then two cross-checks.

**Cross-check 1 — function points.** A count in the region of 2 000 unadjusted FP for this requirement set, at 9–14 hours/FP for an enterprise web build carrying this NFR load, gives 107–167 pm. That sits under the bottom-up figure, as FP routinely does where the non-functional work (four environments, HA/DR, perf tuning, migration, accessibility, IE 9) is a large fraction of the job. It argues for a fat lower tail rather than a lower point.

**Cross-check 2 — reference class.** A three-module B2B portal replacement with migration and an external-facing API, delivered by an outsourcing vendor building its first system in the domain, runs as a 14–18 month program with 11–14 people including QA and PM. At ~0.88 pm per person-calendar-month (leave is outside this convention), 13 × 16 × 0.88 ≈ 183 pm. Consistent.

**What pushes up, what pushes down.** Up: the business rules and standards are undefined at RFP time and get defined inside the project; 25 industries as configuration data still means 25 sets to elicit; the performance targets are specific and will force tuning cycles; IE 9 support (A19 leaves the 2018 era unadjusted) is a real front-end tax. Down: Location reuses Product's chassis heavily, publish/subscribe is one mechanism seen from two sides (A15), and A5–A7, A9, A12, A13 all refuse the expensive readings (no gateway, no monitoring platform, no ERP connectors, no resolver, no CMS, no BI product). The point estimate takes those refusals at face value; the P90 is where one or two of them get overturned in contract negotiation.
