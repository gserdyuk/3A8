## 1. The declaration

### 1.1 Unit
**One unit is one staffed person-month (PM): one full-time person on the vendor's project roster for one calendar month.** It counts recorded, assigned working time. It includes that time's meetings, reviews, coordination, context switching and waiting. It is **not** hours of hands-on work on the task.

- 1 staffed PM ≈ 21.7 working days before leave, or ≈ 18.7 days actually recorded (see 1.2), or ≈ 150 recorded hours at 8 h/day.
- If a reader needs hands-on task hours, the usual practitioner figure is 5–6.5 hands-on hours per recorded day. I do not apply that conversion.

### 1.2 Losses
**Annual leave, public holidays and sickness are inside the figures.** A roster month contains the leave people take during it.

- Conversion used: losses ≈ 14% of working days. That assumes about 20–24 leave days, about 10 holidays and about 5 sick days a year out of 261 working days, typical for a US vendor with Central or Eastern European delivery staff.
- **To remove losses, multiply by 0.86.**
- Reasonable range for the factor: 0.83–0.90, depending on where the team sits.

## 2. The reference class

**Definition.** A new-build (greenfield) enterprise B2B web application that replaces several legacy member-facing applications of a large not-for-profit or standards organisation. It is delivered by an outsourcing vendor as one team at one site, new to the client's domain. **It is estimated at RFP stage**, from high-level requirements before discovery, in a competitive tender.

**Membership criteria**, by the branches of work such projects contain:
1. Several related modules built on one shared data model and service architecture. This means record-management workflows, not a single-purpose tool.
2. Role-based access with multi-tenant company administration, integrated with an external single sign-on or identity provider.
3. A member-facing public API alongside the web UI.
4. Reporting and a separate read store or data mart.
5. **Migration of data and users from several legacy systems.**
6. Enterprise non-functional work: high availability and failover, disaster recovery, performance testing to stated load, a multi-environment deployment path, audit trail, accessibility.
7. A UX redesign ("new look and feel").
8. Requirements and business rules still to be defined inside the project.

**Why this project belongs:** it has all eight branches.

**What it is not**, because this project lacks branches those classes carry:
- **Large ERP or COTS implementation programmes** (COTS: commercial off-the-shelf software). They carry organisational change, finance and HR processes, and many integrations.
- **Regulated or safety-critical systems.** They carry certification and validation.
- **Consumer or mobile products.** They carry native apps, payments and growth experiments.
- **Single-module line-of-business portals.** They lack the migration, API, data-mart and high-availability branches.

**Stage matters.** RFP-stage estimates in competitive tenders show two systematic errors:
- the width of the cone of uncertainty (see 3.2)
- the **winner's curse**: the bid that wins is disproportionately the most optimistic one.

**Size regime:** medium-large business application. As a class judgement, not a count, this is about 1,000–3,000 IFPUG function points (function points are a standard unit of delivered functionality).

## 4. Synthesis

**Route 1: actuals directly (A-1, A-2, A-3).** Central value 200–260 PM, envelope 70–500. These repositories hold **completed projects only**: cancelled and restarted projects are missing, so their upper tail is too thin.

**Route 2: bids × overrun (A-4 × R-2, R-3, R-6).**
- The bid median of ~190 PM, times a median actual-to-estimate ratio of ~1.2–1.3, gives **~230–250 PM**. The median ratio sits below the mean because the distribution is skewed.
- The upper bid (~260) times the R-3 tail and the R-6 winner's-curse effect puts P90 at **~450–500 PM**.

**Agreement and disagreement:**
- **The two routes agree at the median** within about 20% (200–260 vs 230–250). This is the only independent cross-check this method has, and it holds.
- **They disagree in the upper tail.** Route 1 puts ~P90 at 380–430 PM; route 2 at 450–500+. I side with route 2, because route 1's survivor bias is a known and one-directional error.
- **The relative sources disagree among themselves** about the size of the mean overrun, by about ×2.4 (27% vs 66%). This is kept in the spread, not averaged.
- **A-5 (budgets)** covers the whole range and cannot tell the routes apart. I kept it only as a sanity bound.
