## 1. The declaration

**1. Unit.** One unit is one **FTE-month** (one full-time person assigned to the project for one calendar month).
- It is a **recorded, assigned working month**. It includes that month's meetings, reviews, coordination, context switching and waiting.
- It is **not** hours spent on the task itself.
- For hours: about **152 recorded hours per FTE-month**, after the losses in field 2.

**2. Losses.** Annual leave, public holidays and sickness are **inside** the figures, because an assigned head's calendar month absorbs them.
- Conversion: about 21.7 nominal workdays per month, less about 11–12% for losses (about 25 days of leave and holidays plus about 5 sick days out of 260 per year). That leaves about **19 recorded working days, or about 152 h, per FTE-month**.
- To express the figures with losses outside, divide by about 0.885, which gives about 172 nominal hours per FTE-month.

**3. Roles.** Every role on the **vendor's delivery team** that is charged to the project is counted:
- business analysis and working out the business rules
- UX/UI design
- architecture
- development
- QA, including performance and failover testing
- DevOps and environment engineering
- data migration
- project and delivery management
- technical writing for the runbook and handover pack

Explicitly **outside**:
- client staff: subject-matter experts, product owner, UAT testers, the identity-management (IdM) team, 3scale administrators, hosting and operations
- third-party audits (penetration testing, accessibility audit)
- licences, hardware and cloud costs
- pre-sales and bid effort

**4. Source conventions and where they disagree.**

| Anchor family | Native convention | Conversion I did | Inconsistency |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISBSG repository (an international dataset of completed software projects) | Normalised work effort in **recorded hours**, whole lifecycle, project team incl. PM and QA, client effort usually excluded | ÷ 152 h/FTE-month | ISBSG hours are timesheet hours with leave outside, so ÷152 treats a recorded hour as if leave were already netted out. That makes this family read about **5–10% low** against the FTE-month unit. |

| Overrun ratios (Flyvbjerg, McKinsey–Oxford, Jørgensen, Little, Boehm/McConnell) | No unit; many measure **cost** overrun, not effort | Cost overrun treated as a proxy for effort overrun | Cost overrun includes rate and licence changes. ## 2. The reference class

**Definition.** A custom-built, enterprise-grade B2B web application suite that replaces several existing member-facing applications. It is delivered end to end (requirements and design through production cutover) by a large outsourcing vendor with a mixed middle/senior team that is new to the domain. The client is a large member organisation with enterprise decision speed. The estimate is made **at the RFP stage**: high-level requirements, business rules still to be defined inside the project, and a key dependency (the IdM) not yet settled.

**Membership criteria (by structure, not size).** A class member contains all of these branches of work:
1. A discovery, business-rule and UX redesign phase inside the contract.
2. Several transactional modules over a shared data model, with role-based access and approval workflow.
3. A member-facing public API behind an existing API-management product.
4. A separate read or reporting store for search and reports.
5. Integration with an external identity provider.
6. Migration of millions of records and the user base from several legacy applications.
7. Explicit non-functional engineering: high availability and failover, DR, performance testing to stated SLAs, accessibility, audit trail.
8. A multi-environment deployment path, acceptance and cutover.

A class member does **not** carry a post-production run or support term, a package or COTS selection, hardware procurement, or a public-facing data resolver.

**Size regime.** Medium-large: roughly 10–25 average FTE over 12–24 months elapsed. I infer this from the class membership (several modules, public API, migration, NFR branch), not from counting features.

**Stage.** This is the RFP stage, before discovery. The error at this stage is larger and more one-sided than after discovery: the cone of uncertainty (how wide the estimate range is at each stage of a project) is at its widest, and in a competitive bid the winning estimate tends to be the most optimistic one.

**Neighbouring classes this project is not in:**
- **Portal refresh over existing back-end services.** That class lacks the new data architecture, the read store and the migration branches. It is lighter.
- **Data-platform or master-data modernisation** at a standards body, where the data-sharing platform itself is the product. That class carries whole data-governance and syndication branches that this RFP puts in "future expansion". It is heavier.
- **Configuring a product-information package plus a custom portal.** That class has a different mix of work.

## 4. Synthesis

- **Actuals-based anchors** (A-2, A-3) centre on about **230–280 FTE-months**, with a wide band of 120–490.
- **Bid-based anchors** (A-1) centre on about **230–250**. After the relative anchors (R-2, R-4, R-6: median about 1.3–1.5× for an RFP-stage competitive bid), they imply actuals of about **300–350**.
- **The two families disagree by about 25–35% at the centre.** The direction makes sense: actuals-based anchors are an average over all project types and stages, while the bid-derived path carries the RFP-stage and winner's-curse bias explicitly. Neither family explains the gap away, so I place the median between them, leaning towards the bid-derived path because the stage markers here are strong.
- **Spread.** R-1 and R-5 both point to a P90/P10 ratio of about 3. The two absolute families also overlap only partly, which is honest evidence of variation within the class. R-3 means the tail beyond P90 is fatter than a lognormal.
- **Cross-check.** The independent anchors agree on the **shape** (right skew, median above the bid) and on the **order of magnitude** (hundreds of FTE-months, not tens and not thousands). They do not agree tightly on the **number**.
