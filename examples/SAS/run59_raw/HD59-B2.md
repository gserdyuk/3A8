## 5. Doubts

**Enumerations that could honestly be one item longer or shorter**

1. **Shared obligations, stated reading.** The alternative is to count every covered obligation in full on every element that covers it: NFR-16 on A008, A020 and A165; G-1, G-1.2 and G-3.1 on A013, A014 and A015; NFR-3 on A016, A017, A025, A146 and A147. That would push several of these elements up by one or more classes.
2. **A007:** "functions are shared across modules" could count separately from permission-based availability. That gives 4, which is L.
3. **A008:** validating auto-generated and manual identifiers could be two actions. That gives 4, which is L.
4. **A009:** Standards rules and business rules could be one "validation rule" kind. That gives 1, which is S.
5. **A013:** designating Editor and Approver could be instances of "assign roles to users". That gives 1, which is S.
6. **A014:** the Editor/Approver designations are counted on both A013 and A014. If they belong only to A013, A014 gives 1, which is S. I name the overlap and do not merge anything.
7. **A015:** the company-scope confinement could be read as part of the designation, giving 1 (S). Alternatively each conferred ability could count, giving up to 5 (L).
8. **A017:** customer-level roles (G-1.4) and company-level roles could be separate kinds. That gives 2, which is M.
9. **A018:** function scope and data scope could be one "permission set" kind. That gives 1, which is S.
10. **A020:** status assignment "by the system or the user" could be two actions, giving 7 (XL, which would also be a coarseness finding). Separate workflow entry for new and edited records (G-3) would add one more.
11. **A024:** the three initiators could be one "reset password" action with different actors. That gives 1, which is S.
12. **A025 / A146:** OAuth and SAML could be one "claims-based standards" concern. Each element drops by one; neither class changes.
13. **A026:** releasing the lock when editing ends is implied by "one user at a time". Adding it gives 3, class unchanged. Folding the refusal into the lock gives 1, which is S.
14. **A046:** the name token "configured address" could count as a "configure feedback destination" action. That gives 4, which is L.
15. **A047:** viewing industry-specific help could be a separate user task. That gives 2, which is M.
16. **A049:** "maintained" could be split into create, edit and retire, which would give M. The obligation does not name those parts, so I kept it as one.
17. **A050:** the three link types could be one "training link" kind with a type attribute. That gives 1, which is S.
18. **A147:** mapping to company, user and role could be one mapping action. That gives 1, which is S.
19. **A165:** UI-entered and imported attributes could be one verification action. That gives 1, which is S.
20. **C02:** "session management" could be read as a catch-all under P-5. That gives 1, which is S.
21. **C01:** three of its triggers (A041, A069, A137) are outside this batch, so it was counted from its name alone. Their content could add a kind.
22. **Special-count reading.** My engine definition describes the store special count as pre-loading ("must exist before the system is usable"). The pinned rules describe it as loading from predecessor applications. I used the pinned rules. Under the pre-loading reading, A009, A018, A048, A050 and C01 would have non-empty lists.

**Contradictions between obligations (named, not resolved)**

23. **Where users and roles live.** NFR-3 says "user accounts and roles defined in an enterprise Identity Management system". G-1.1, G-1.2, G-1.3 and G-3.1 say users are added and edited, and roles catalogued and assigned, inside the application. I counted both as written.
24. **Who sets record status.** P-10.2 and L-9.2 say "the system or the user can assign a status to each record". NFR-16 and G-3 say status moves through a workflow: submitted, reviewed, rejected or approved. Free status assignment would bypass that workflow. I counted both as written.
