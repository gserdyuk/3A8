### Shape beyond P90

I do **not** continue a lognormal past P90. Between P10 and P90 the class behaves close to lognormal with σ ≈ 0.55; a pure lognormal with that σ would put P95 at 2.47×P50 and P99 at 3.59×P50. I have lifted both — P95 to 2.71 and P99 to 4.43 — because the best-evidenced finding about IT cost outcomes is that the extreme tail is heavier than lognormal, closer to a power law: the catastrophic cases are not "a bad version of the normal case," they are a different regime that a bad run enters and cannot leave. Flyvbjerg & Budzier's result that roughly one project in six exceeds its budget by 200% or more is not reproducible from a σ = 0.55 lognormal at all; the tail has to be fattened by hand or the shape is simply wrong.

So the declared shape is: **lognormal body, fattened tail, no upper truncation applied by me** (see the truncation caveat below, which cuts the other way).

### Class scenario at P95 — 380 person-months

The run of events that puts a project of this class here is not one disaster but a *chain in the verification phase*. The class signature: the build finishes roughly on the class's median trajectory, and then the project fails to close. Parallel operation against the legacy system produces disagreements that are individually small and collectively unbounded — the two systems differ on edge cases nobody documented, and each difference must be adjudicated (is the old system right, or was it always wrong?) before anyone can decide who is defective. Because the definition of done in this class includes *decommissioning the predecessor*, the project cannot be declared finished while any disagreement is open, and the cost of the phase becomes a function of the legacy system's undocumented behaviour rather than of the new system's design. Typical accompanying facts at this quantile: the team is not reduced after "feature complete" because it cannot be, the discovery phase turns out to have selected an architecture that meets the nominal target but not the burst target, and a partial redesign is absorbed mid-flight rather than restarted. Readable as roughly 13 people for 29 months, or 12 for 32, all roles.

### Class scenario at P99 — 620 person-months

At this quantile the class scenario is a **restart or a de facto second project**. One of three patterns, in rough order of frequency in the class: (a) the chosen mechanism for the system's central hard property — in this class, guaranteed delivery and state tracking under failure — is found to be unfixable at target load after substantial investment, and is rebuilt from a different premise while the first is still in the tree; (b) the parallel run never converges and the organisation ends up funding two systems and a reconciliation team for a year or more; (c) key-person departure in a team that was new to the domain, which in this class costs more than elsewhere because the domain knowledge was acquired, not hired, and lives in very few heads. These runs typically also show a change of engineering leadership. Readable as roughly 16 people for 39 months, or as the sum of an abandoned attempt and a completed one.

### The truncation caveat, stated where it belongs

My P99 is the class's estimate of **effort the project would consume if allowed to run**. It is not necessarily the effort such a project *is recorded as having consumed*, because the class contains a truncation mechanism that operates precisely in the tail: a product company of this size cannot fund a 600-person-month rework of a system it already owns without existential consequences, so the tail is cut by management action — descope, declare the v2 "good enough for the new customers only," keep the old system alive indefinitely, or stop. This is blind-spot 6 on my static list and I flag it explicitly here rather than adjust for it, because adjusting would require knowing what this sponsor actually did, which is exactly what the outside view cannot see. **Direction of the bias: my P95 and P99 are probably too high as predictors of recorded effort, and about right as predictors of required effort.** These two diverge only above P90; below it they coincide.

## 3. UNIT DISCLOSURE

This section answers the second registered question. It is the part of the output most likely to explain a divergence from another sensor, so it is stated at length.

### What one unit is

**My unit is the funded-seat person-month, and my person-day is a recorded, assigned working day — not pure hours of task work.**

Inside one of my person-days: that day's standups, planning and review meetings, code review given and received, design discussion, context switching, waiting on other people, reading, rework, and the ordinary friction of a working day. I have applied **no focus factor**. If a consumer of this forecast wants "hours of hands-on task work," they must deflate my figures themselves — the customary factor is 0.5 to 0.7, and I did not apply it in either direction. If a consumer's own bottom-up numbers are built from *ideal engineering days*, my numbers are on a different scale from theirs and comparing them directly will manufacture a divergence that is purely a units artefact of roughly 1.4×–2×.

### Leave, holidays, sickness

**Inside.** A person on the team for twelve months costs twelve person-months in my unit whether or not they took four weeks off. This follows necessarily from how my dominant anchor is built: the anchor is headcount × calendar duration, i.e. funded seats over elapsed time, and a funded seat is paid through its holidays.

Consequences for conversion:
- 1 person-month = **21.7 recorded weekdays** (260.9 weekdays per year ÷ 12), which is the factor I used for the person-day column.
- The same month contains only about **19.3 days actually worked**, after ~10 US public holidays, ~10–15 vacation days and ~5 sick days per year (≈230–235 available days of 261).

### Whose hours

**Every role charged to the project.** Developers, QA, PM, tech lead, and the attributable share of architect / DBA / build-and-release. This is forced by A3 ("blended composition, including QA and PM") and by the anchor: my team-size base rate of 5–12 FTE is a *whole-team* rate from the class, not a developer headcount. If another sensor reports developer-only effort, its number should be roughly **60–75%** of mine before any comparison is meaningful.

Explicitly **outside** my figures: the PoP/telecom operations staff, data-centre and hardware operations, the third-party OCR and rendering vendors' own engineering, executives, sales and marketing, and all post-launch operation (excluded by A1).

### The convention used by my sources — and the inconsistency between them

This is where the sources genuinely disagree, and the disagreement is not negligible.

| Source family | Convention | Leave inside? |
|---|---|---|
| Boehm / COCOMO II | person-month = 152 hours of *nominal* working time, explicitly net of holidays, vacation and sick leave | **No** |
| QSM / SLIM and most commercial benchmark repositories | staff-months of charged time, closer to payroll; collection practice varies by contributor | Usually yes, inconsistently |
| Practitioner "headcount × calendar months" (my dominant anchor) | funded seats over elapsed time | **Yes** |

The gap between the COCOMO convention and the funded-seat convention is **about 11–13%** — 261 weekdays against ~232 available days. That is the size of the inconsistency, and it is not large enough to explain a serious divergence between sensors on its own, but it is large enough that it should not be absorbed silently.

**What I converted:** nothing. I report in the funded-seat convention because that is the convention of the anchor carrying my level, and converting a well-understood anchor into a foreign convention to match a weaker one would be false precision. **A reader wishing to compare my numbers against a COCOMO-convention figure should multiply mine by ~0.88.**

I chose this convention because it is the one my level actually came from, not because it is convenient. It is in fact the *less* convenient choice: it makes my numbers about 12% larger than the alternative, with no gain to me.

## 4. The reference class

### Definition

> **Replacement (v2) of an already-operating revenue-bearing back-end system, built in-house by a product company's own blended team, where the system's central engineering difficulty is sustained-throughput distributed processing with per-item delivery guarantees, in a pre-cloud era with hand-built infrastructure, by a team new to the business domain, with a definition of done that requires parallel operation against the predecessor and its decommissioning.**

### Membership criteria — structural, not by size

I admit a project to this class if the following categories of work are **present**:

1. A discovery / technology-selection phase driven by unfamiliarity with the domain rather than by unclear requirements.
2. Distributed processing infrastructure built by hand — scheduling, liveness, retry, state tracking — because no off-the-shelf component of the era supplies it at the required trust level.
3. Integration with a set of third-party conversion/processing engines whose behaviour must be stabilised rather than merely called.
4. An operations console for a fleet of machines the team does not physically control.
5. An external-facing user surface.
6. An ingest path from an uncontrolled external protocol.
7. Capture of transactional records for a downstream financial consumer that is out of scope.
8. Verification by parallel run against a live predecessor with output comparison, and cutover.

And the following categories are **absent** — this is what keeps the class tight:

- No work below the application layer: no protocol stacks, no board-level or firmware work, no carrier interconnect.
- No optimisation/decision engine of its own.
- No billing or financial engine.
- No requirements elicitation from scratch with external stakeholders — a running predecessor serves as the specification of record.
- No regulatory certification programme.
- No client-side product (desktop, mobile, installer).
- No mass migration of a historical data corpus as a distinct programme.
- No post-launch operations period.

### Why this project is in it

It has all eight present-categories and none of the eight absent-categories: A4 removes the edge software, the routing engine and billing; A1 removes operations; A3 supplies the running predecessor as the specification of record. A5 places it firmly in the pre-cloud regime where category 2 is hand-built rather than configured, which is the class's defining cost driver.

### Which neighbouring class it is **not** in, and the confidence split

| Class | Confidence | If this is really the right class, the answer moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Stated class** — pre-cloud v2 replacement with hand-built distributed infrastructure and parallel-run cutover | **55%** | — |
| **Greenfield distributed platform at scale** (no predecessor to specify against, no parallel run) | 15% | **Up.** Loses the specification-of-record benefit, gains full requirements churn; median up ~25–40%, tail fatter. |
| **Enterprise application v2** (portal + background workers, throughput not actually a binding constraint) | 15% | **Down.** If the real traffic never approached the design target and the team knew it, category 2 collapses from an engineering programme to a component; median down ~35–45%. Note A6 fixes the design target, so this neighbour requires the design target to have been nominal in practice. |
| **Incremental modernisation / strangler-pattern rework** (predecessor code partially reused, cutover piecewise) | 10% | **Down.** Removes the all-or-nothing cutover risk that generates most of this class's tail; median down ~20%, P90/P50 falls toward 1.6. A4 argues against membership. |
| **Full telecom service platform** including edge, routing and billing | 5% | **Up sharply,** 2–3×. Excluded by A4, retained at 5% only because scope boundaries recalled 17 years later are the least reliable part of any such account. |

**Misclassification is the largest single source of error in this forecast**, larger than the sampling error inside the stated class. The two "down" neighbours together carry 25% and the two "up" neighbours 20%, so the mis-specification risk is roughly balanced in direction but wide in magnitude — which is one of the reasons my P10–P90 span is a factor of four rather than a factor of two.

### Stage of estimation

This is the stage disclosure the method requires, and it is unusual for this project.

The information available is at **RFP / pre-discovery granularity** — a scope list, a scale target, an era, a team shape. No requirements, no design, no decomposition. In pure information terms that is the widest point of the cone of uncertainty at which anyone estimates seriously.

But the *incentive structure* of the usual RFP stage is absent: there is no bid to win, no sponsor whose expectation has been voiced, no competitor to underbid. The systematic optimism that makes RFP-stage estimates biased low is therefore weakened here.

In its place sits a different systematic bias, pointing the same way: **the scope was recalled, not documented.** Accounts recorded years later are reliably under-inclusive — they retain the interesting work and lose the glue, the second integration of the same thing, the environment work, the data fix-ups. The participant's own uncertainty markers ("on the order of 8–10 formats", "probably 20, maybe 16", "possibly Australia") are visible evidence of this, and they are markers on the items the participant *did* remember. Items forgotten entirely carry no markers at all.

Net effect on my output: I have kept the full RFP-stage width, and I have placed the median in the **upper half** of the class band rather than at its centre. That placement is a stage adjustment, not a feature adjustment.

## 6. Synthesis

The two anchor families **agree on shape and cannot cross-check level**, and this asymmetry is the most important thing in the forecast.

**On shape they agree, and the agreement is genuine.** R3 puts the body at ×0.5–×2 around the central case. R2's lognormal with σ ≈ 0.55 independently produces P10/P50 = 0.49 and P90/P50 = 2.02. R1 independently insists that whatever the body looks like, the region past P90 is heavier than the body's distribution predicts. Three sources of different provenance — a practitioner synthesis of estimation practice, a systematic review of the empirical literature, and a large-sample study of outcomes — converge on the same figure of merit. **This is a real cross-check and it passed.** It also matches this project's own recorded finding that class-derived shapes are the stable part.

**On level they do not agree, because only one of them has a level at all.** R1–R6 are all *ratios*; they multiply something but supply nothing to multiply. Every person-month in my output descends from A3, which is A1 × A2: 5–12 FTE times 12–24 months. Anchors A4, A5, A6 and A7 sharpen the placement *within* A3's band and shape the tail, but not one of them could produce a magnitude on its own without a decomposition, which this sensor is forbidden.

**Therefore: my shape is triple-sourced and my level is single-sourced.** If this forecast is wrong, the most probable failure is that A1 or A2 is wrong for this project — a team of 4 rather than 8, or a run of 30 months rather than 17 — and not that the ratios are wrong. Anyone reconciling this sensor against another should attack the level first and leave the ratios alone; the ratios are the part I would defend.

The placement decision, stated once and plainly: A3's arithmetic centre is ~135 PM. Three considerations push right (A4's systems-software multiplier, A7's pre-cloud penalty, R6's recall incompleteness), one pushes left (the specification-of-record benefit of having a running v1, already partly credited by placing the project in the stated class rather than in the greenfield neighbour). Net: **P50 = 140 PM**, in the upper half of the band, a modest displacement. I resisted a larger displacement because each of the three rightward considerations is an anchor whose *number* I rated low or absent, and stacking three low-confidence numbers into a big move is exactly how outside views get quietly converted into inside ones.
