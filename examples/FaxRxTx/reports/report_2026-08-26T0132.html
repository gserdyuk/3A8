What the corridor's pass does and does not mean

The corridor rests on one declared number — the correlation between work items, ρ = 0.5. That number was not fitted on this case: it was declared while working the other case, on the general reasoning that the truth is neither 0 nor 1 and far from both. But it was declared with this outcome already on the record and known. So it is not fitted, and it is not blind. A corridor whose width parameter is chosen after the answer is visible is weaker evidence than one chosen before, and this report says so rather than counting the pass at full weight.

And the case has stopped being able to score anything further

Three conditions of the case arrived from the outcome's owner after the estimate existed: staffed-versus-delivered time, the presence fraction, and the headcount on the concept stage. Each was legitimate — each was a real input nobody had asked for. The last is one sixth of the answer by arithmetic: ten people for two of twelve months. A gate cleared by being handed a sixth of the answer is not a gate.

The rule the episode produced, and it costs nothing to apply from now on: a case profile is pinned with the requirements, before any estimate — team grade and domain experience, declared overheads and what is inside them, the presence fraction, the process, and the staffing of every stage the method prices separately. All of it was obtainable on day one, from the same person. A case whose conditions arrive after its number can be learned from; it cannot score.

One parameter still undeclared, and it is on the outcome's side

The outcome is recorded as 120 staffed person-months — ten people on the staff for about a year, from a participant's memory, stated at ±20%. Turning that into working time needs the presence fraction: how much of a nominal staffed year is actually present. At 0.88 the outcome is 1663 person-days and the miss is ×1.17; across the plausible 0.83–0.91 it runs ×1.10 to ×1.21. The verdict does not turn on it, but the figure is not declared and should be.

The last rung is how many of a present day's eight nominal hours are work on the task. What comes out inside the day: standups, planning and review meetings, code review given and received, design discussion, context switching, waiting on other people, reading, rework, ordinary friction. This project declares 6. The sensor declares 4.0–5.6. That is the whole of the disagreement, and it is worth ×1.25 on where the blue curve sits — nothing else in the ladder is open.

That is the same conversion the outcome is placed by, which is why the class and the outcome keep their exact ratio of ×1.17 whatever convention is chosen — both are funded seats, and the factor cancels. It does not cancel against the bottom-up, which is native to task hours: at the sensor's own 4.8 hours the blue curve would sit at 1552 rather than 1940, left of the outcome instead of right of it.

What the blue curve actually is

It is the sensor's own declared body — a lognormal with σ = 0.55 — and not an interpolation between its quantiles. That distinction matters: interpolating between stated points produces a flat block between P10 and P50 and destroys the mode, so the curve comes out looking like a descending ramp rather than a distribution. The declared lognormal reproduces the sensor's own P10, P80 and P90 to within 1–2%, and puts the mode at about 1434 — left of the median, as a right-skewed body has it.

Two honest edges. Nothing below P10 was stated, and the lognormal supplies that region from its own shape rather than from anything the sensor said. And the stated P95 sits about 9% above the lognormal, because the sensor lifted its tail deliberately, saying the extreme tail of IT outcomes is heavier than lognormal and giving its reason; the drawn curve does not carry that lift.

The class it constructed

Not looked up — built for this project at run time, with membership stated as categories of work that must be present and categories that must be absent:

Replacement of an already-operating revenue-bearing back-end system, built in-house by a product company's own blended team, where the central engineering difficulty is sustained-throughput distributed processing with per-item delivery guarantees, in a pre-cloud era with hand-built infrastructure, by a team new to the business domain, with a definition of done that requires parallel operation against the predecessor and its decommissioning.

55% confidence in that class, with 20 points of the remainder moving the answer up and 25 points moving it down. It named misclassification as the largest single source of error in its own forecast — larger than the sampling error inside the class.

Its shape is triple-sourced; its level is single-sourced — and it says so

The ratios P10/P50 = 0.50 and P90/P50 = 2.07 fall out of three sources of different provenance that agree: the published cone of uncertainty, a systematic review of estimation studies, and a large-sample study of outcomes. Every person-month of the level, by contrast, descends from one anchor — team size multiplied by calendar duration.

If this forecast is wrong, the most probable failure is that A1 or A2 is wrong for this project, and not that the ratios are wrong. Anyone reconciling this sensor against another should attack the level first and leave the ratios alone.

The contamination residue it declared against itself

The input carried the calendar window — "roughly 2007–2009" — and its principal anchor is built as team × duration, so a duration hint is a live path into its level. It raised this unprompted, bounded the possible nudge at ±15% on the median, said it could not bound it tighter, and recommended stripping the window from any future run. The duration band it used, 12–24 months, is not pulled toward the hint; that is a fact about its declared anchor, not a measure of how much the hint mattered.

And a tail with its own truncation caveat

It refused to continue a lognormal past P90, on the ground that the extreme tail of IT outcomes is heavier than lognormal, and lifted P95 and P99 by hand. Then it cut against itself: a product company of this size cannot fund a 620-person-month rework of a system it already owns, so the class's own economics delete the project rather than fund it.

My P95 and P99 are probably too high as predictors of recorded effort, and about right as predictors of required effort. These two diverge only above P90; below it they coincide.

03How to read the chartthe method

One distribution and one line. The line is what happened; the distribution is what the method said before anyone looked.

Where the distribution comes from

Every rate cell carries an optimistic, a most-likely and a pessimistic value. The chain collapsed each to its expected value before summing, so the range existed at the leaf and was destroyed at the total. Restoring it needs one declared number: how strongly the 543 priced items are correlated. Both ends are wrong — independent gives a P10–P90 of ×1.04, which would claim the total is known to ±1%; perfectly correlated gives ×1.86. ρ = 0.5 is declared, and across ρ from 0.25 to 0.75 the band edges move about ±20%.

ΣO…ΣP — every optimistic and every pessimistic end summed — spans ×4.14 here. That is six sigma, the absolute extremes, not a percentile band; a P10–P90 is 2.56 sigma.

Why there is no outside view on this chart

The reference-class sensor has not run on this case in this generation. It produced two readings for it in 2026 — and both are in units that were never declared, which is why the four cross-generation readings that rested on them are currently suspended rather than quoted. Drawing them here would put quantities of unknown dimension on a scale with a known one.

The consequence is worth stating plainly: this case has no second opinion. The one number on this chart comes from one method, and what checks it is not another instrument but the outcome.

The unit

Person-days of 8 net hours of work on the task, 40 to the week. Leave, public holidays and sickness are not in these numbers, and neither is any notion of calendar or headcount. If you need days of presence, divide by the effective task hours your working day delivers — commonly 5 to 6, never 8 — and add your own allowance for leave.

That unit cost this project three days and three separate errors, all the same shape: the outcome recorded as headcount × calendar and read as delivered work; a conversion applied to one side of the comparison only, worth ×1.33 in the estimator's own favour; and the rate table's hour content relabelled without re-derivation, a relabelling its own author declined to certify. The unit of a comparison is part of the comparison, and it is an input on both sides.

Known direction of error

The curve is a normal approximation. Rate cells are right-skewed and correlated sums keep skew, so the true upper tail is fatter than drawn.
