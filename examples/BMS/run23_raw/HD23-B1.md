## 5. Doubts

Band-changing ambiguities first — these are where a second reader would most plausibly land
elsewhere.

1. **N34, XL vs L.** I counted the 8 stage tokens as 8 distinct transition actions. Read as the 5
   *stage groups* the requirement punctuates ("Proposed", "Accepted/Rejected", "Approved/Declined",
   "Booked/Cancelled", "Paid") the count is 5 → **L**. I chose 8 because "Accepted" and "Rejected"
   are different actions on the same object, not one action. This is the single largest sizing risk
   in the batch and the only XL.
2. **N43, M vs S.** "Pick-up and drop-off locations" may be **two** entity kinds or **one** location
   kind held in two roles. One kind → **S**, and the pre-load special count moves to **S** with it.
3. **N41, M vs S.** If "One transport supplier (Uber) is integrated automatically" is read as a
   restatement of "Automatic booking" specialised to Uber rather than as a routing decision, the
   count is 1 → **S**.
4. **Name tokens counted as actions.** "normalised", "cached" (N85), "ranking", "recalculation"
   (N72), "Orchestration", "Fallback" (N73) were counted, since the rule admits the element's name
   as substance. Excluding them: N85 → 3 (**M**), N72 → 3 (**M**), N73 → 1 (**S**). This one
   decision moves three of the four largest elements in the batch. It is the most load-bearing
   interpretive choice I made, and I flag it as such.

Doubts about the work model itself, priced as given regardless:

5. **N37 contradicts its own sibling's obligation.** N37 is "Automatic Hotel Booking"; N38 covers
   R38 "Hotel supplier booking is handled manually". If hotel booking is manual, N37 may be work
   that should not exist. Sized S anyway; I may not remove it.
6. **R29 "Automatic booking" is covered three times** — N73, N37, N41. Three elements act on one
   obligation. Not an error I can correct, but any downstream roll-up should know the same
   obligation is carried three times.
7. **R40 and R48 read as duplicates** — "All other transport suppliers are manual" and "Transport
   supplier booking is handled manually" name the same thing; both land on N42 and neither adds a
   task.
8. **N21 UPSA is my least-confident sizing.** The substance names a binding and nothing else — no
   operation, no purpose. Sized S on the protocol-counts-as-one clause. If UPSA integration means
   several operations, this element is understated by a wide margin, and nothing in the batch would
   reveal it.
9. **N22 hides a fan-out behind one name.** "multiple third-party systems" is plural and
   unenumerated; only the word "Aggregator" in the element name keeps the count at 1. If those
   systems are reached directly, the true count is unbounded.
10. **N16's mixed kind.** R61 is compliance-shaped, R66 behavioural. I declared the element
    behavioural. A reader who splits the element by kind would get two different readings.
11. **N17/N18 declared unsizeable** rather than forced to S — see §4. A reader who insists every
    non-aggregate carries a class will disagree with this and should say so explicitly rather than
    quietly filling the hole.
