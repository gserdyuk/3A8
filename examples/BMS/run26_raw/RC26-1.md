## 1. The reference class

*Greenfield first release of a bespoke, single-tenant enterprise workflow-and-integration web
system, built and hosted by an outsourced professional-services supplier for one large corporate
client, estimated at RFP stage (pre-discovery, requirements written by the client and not yet
analysed by the supplier).*

Membership criteria (structural, all present): multiple role-facing front ends over one domain
model · a stateful business process with approvals, transitions, notification fan-out · a small
handful of external interfaces (enterprise APIs, an identity protocol, 1–2 commercial aggregator
APIs, a commodity messaging gateway) · client-configurable rules · operational reporting ·
supplier-built hosting and multi-environment pipeline · first release only (run/operate carved out
by A1).

Categories absent (and keeping it out of neighbours): high-volume throughput engineering (~6000
transactions/year is trivial — not the booking-platform class) · legacy migration · multi-tenancy /
billing / productization · native mobile · ML/optimisation research · certification programme ·
training/warranty/run.

Stage is part of the class: at RFP stage the dominant error is unresolved ambiguity, and in this
class ambiguities resolve against the supplier more often than for it. Size regime: one team, one
release train (~6 effective FTE) — below the multi-team-programme regime, above the
fixed-costs-dominate regime.

## 2. Base rates and anchors (condensed to values · source · confidence)

Absolute: **A** team×duration — median ≈ 9 months at ~6 effective FTE × ~18 net pd/person-month →
**P50 ≈ 970 pd, band 540–1900** (practitioner consensus + ISBSG durations; shape high, number
medium) · **B** size×productivity — 400–900 FP (median ≈ 600) × 12–14 h/FP median (P25 7, P75 25) →

market; low-medium).

Relative: **R1** overrun mean ≈ +27%, fat tail, 1-in-6 > +200% (Flyvbjerg & Budzier; tail
down-weighted for this size regime — "a $0.4M project gets cancelled or cut; a $167M programme gets
funded to completion") · **R2** Standish challenged-share (number low, methodology criticised) ·
**R3** cone at this stage ≈ 0.5×–2×, real spread wider on the high side · **R4** requirements creep
1–2%/month → 10–20% over a 9-month build (Capers Jones) · **R5** RFP-stage supplier estimate →
delivered ×1.2–1.5 at median, after normal de-scoping · **R6** ambiguity premium +20–50% where
integration contracts and NFR criteria are undefined at RFP.

## 3. Synthesis

A and B are actuals-based and already contain the class overrun — they fix the **median**; relative
anchors shape spread and skew only (applying them on top would double-count). C is bid-based and
converts via R5 to **1080–1575** — 10–60% above A/B. Read as: the 9-month duration median has
already absorbed some slip, so the median is placed in the **upper part of the A/B region: P50 =
1050, not 970**. A and B landing within 1% of each other (970 vs ~975) from unrelated quantities is
"same order, same central region", not precision.

Skew: everything that departs the median departs upward; hard floor ≈ 500 pd (several front ends +
counterparty interfaces + production hand-over must exist); right tail bounded only by management
intervention.
