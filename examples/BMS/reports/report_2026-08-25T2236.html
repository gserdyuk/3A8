01How to read the chartthe method

Both instruments produce a distribution. Only one of them did so without being asked.

The outside view produces quantiles natively; that is what the method is. The bottom-up can too, and always could: every rate cell carries an optimistic, a most-likely and a pessimistic value. The chain collapsed each cell to its expected value before summing, so the range existed at the leaf and was destroyed at the total. That is why this project reported a centre and no corridor for so long.

Restoring it needs exactly one declared number

The width of a sum of 505 items depends on how strongly those items are correlated, and nothing in the data says. Both ends are wrong: independent gives a P10–P90 of ×1.04, which would claim the total is known to ±1%; perfectly correlated gives ×1.87. ρ = 0.5 is declared, on the reasoning that the truth is certainly neither and far from both. It matters less than it looks — across ρ from 0.25 to 0.75, a threefold range, the band edges move about ±20%.

A distinction worth keeping straight

ΣO…ΣP — every optimistic end and every pessimistic end summed — spans ×4.19 and was long quoted as the bottom-up's honest band. It is not a percentile band: it is six sigma, the absolute extremes. A P10–P90 is 2.56 sigma. Even at perfect correlation the 80% interval is ×1.87, not ×4.19.

The three outside-view readings are not three repeats

A and B are repeats of one another — same input, same day. C was made three days later with one assumption withheld and an additional question asked, so part of the distance between C and the pair may be that difference rather than the sensor's own spread. All three were produced without ever seeing the product model, the work items or the rate table.

Only the marked quantiles are readings; the curve between them is interpolation. A and B were asked for P10, P50, P80, P90 and a floor and stop there — their tails are hatched rather than drawn, because inventing a P95 is the move this method exists to prevent. C was asked for the tail and answered: P95 = 3450, P99 = 6375 and off the scale. It declined to give an unconditional P99 at all, on the ground that beyond a point the project is stopped and restarted and effort ceases to be the right unit for what happened.

The unit, and the one conversion applied

Person-days of 8 net hours of work on the task, 40 to the week. Leave, public holidays and sickness are not in these numbers, and neither is any notion of calendar or headcount.

The declared premise: time in an estimate by analogy contains the losses to meetings, and is already clean of leave and days off. All three readings confirmed this of themselves in writing, each reasoning in assigned working days and each flagging the mismatch with the unit label it was given. Their own conversions to strict 8-hour task-days: A ×0.8, B ×0.75–0.85, C ×0.56–0.75. The house factor 0.75 is applied to all three; the bottom-up needs none, having been priced in task hours to begin with.

Known direction of error

The bottom-up curve is a normal approximation. Rate cells are right-skewed and correlated sums keep skew, so the true upper tail is fatter than drawn — which moves it further from the outside view, not closer.

02Why the methods disagree×1.49 – ×3.17

At equal scope the calibrated bottom-up sits at ~P75 of one reading and above P90 of the other two. Steps B–D attributed the gap without closing it, and reported the explained share as 0% in those words.

the class
vs itself
The largest single quantity in the comparison ×2.12
The three outside-view medians span 600 to 1275 net days — wider than the distance from the bottom-up to any one of them. Before any unit conversion the raw medians are 800, 1050 and 1700 recorded days, so the conversion did not create it. Part of it may be reading C's changed input, and n = 1 on that input cannot separate the two.
D1
The recursive assembly convention
Integration priced at 20% of each subtree's leaf effort at all 18 parents — which delivers 40.9% of leaf effort, because a deep leaf enters every ancestor's base. It is the method's only free constant. And it cannot carry the gap: deleting it entirely still leaves the calibrated figure ×1.08 to ×2.29 above the outside-view medians.
D4
Class assignment
Every reading flagged its own most likely error as upward. Reading C put 60% on the stated class and split the remaining 40% asymmetrically — 24 points move the answer up, 16 down, and the up-moves are larger. Misclassification adds right-skew on top of the class's own.
D6
Unit provenance settled
Net versus recorded time, carried as two forks and applied in neither direction. Resolved against the bottom-up: the chain prices task-solving hours, the outside view reasons from recorded project time and converts at 0.75, and the gap widened rather than closing. Leave is deliberately not deducted — COCOMO's 152-hour person-month and every timesheet-based dataset removed it already.

What Step C adds, and why the outside view already has it

  • H1, H2, H3 — the three named holes, added outright. The bottom-up refused to size them; the outside view includes them without knowing they exist, because it never decomposes.
  • G1 ×1.15 — scope volatility: obligations beyond the pinned list, RFP-stage under-specification, reading-change churn.
  • G2 ×1.12 — cross-company coordination friction. The assumption log states outright that this is visible only to the outside view.
  • T1 — external-interface realisation risk, on the eight interface elements and their contract tests only, never on the integration line.
  • A-RAMP — the forming deficit of a new team: mobilisation is priced once, the productivity loss is not.
Full convergence after Step C is not necessarily a success. If the methods answer structurally different questions yet converge to a single point, it is either a coincidence, or one method was forced under another and lost its diagnostic independence.

False convergence was checked in three frames and refused in all three — including a seductive near-match within 3%, flagged as exactly what a unit artefact looks like when it imitates agreement.

08Where every number came from6 roles

Each sensor is an engine with a version, stamped on its own output. They are hired for what they are forbidden to see, not for autonomy.

Each element classified by counting named things, the enumeration being the justification. Refuses to run if shown any rate or price. Repeat agreement ×1.008 on the total.
4

External norms, gap-blind: never saw a project total, a budget, or a gap a rate would explain. Calibrated against no outcome yet — the centre is norms passed through a measured size vector, not a validated cost.
5
Outside view ×3
Ran in parallel and in ignorance of all of the above. Every reading declined, unprompted, to read the repository whose path it could see, each naming anchoring as the reason. One also quarantined the obligation count it was given, on the ground that a count is a residue of decomposition.
6
Steps B, C, D
Calibration parameters from a gap-blind source that never saw the outside view or the size of the gap; then the diagnosis, the only participant permitted to see more than one method's output.

Two coordinates, not one

An estimate is a property of the triple (project × engine × model). The model coordinate was measured at ×2.02 against version steps of +0.4% to +30% — it outweighs the entire version history — so a batch on a different model is a different instrument, not a replication.

Protocol findings, recorded rather than quietly fixed

A stale sensor definition loaded in one run; and the launch harness prepends repository status and commit subjects to every sensor, which each sensor has caught and quarantined by itself. That is a workaround, not a mechanism.
