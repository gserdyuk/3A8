## W5 findings

1. **Four in-scope activities rest on a single element each.** K3 and A9 exist only because N16 is a
   statement with a performance property; G1, G2, G3 exist only because N43 is a store. "If either
   classification is revised, up to five activities become unused and the declaration's data and
   statement dimensions are shown to have no purchase on this product. This is the most fragile joint
   in the crossing."
2. **Six of 27 elements are interfaces and the declaration mandates no assembly activity.** Integration
   is priced downstream at aggregation nodes — so N19 and the out-of-scope root carry integration work
   this crossing does not represent. "Either the technology is right and the downstream aggregation
   must be told, or the technology is wrong for a product whose integration layer is its largest
   subtree."
3. **The product model is not a partition over these obligations.** R29 is covered by three elements,
   R15/R26/R27/R41/R61 by two each, so the projection rows overlap and do not sum.
4. **N84 spans more than one thing** — the failure behaviour of all six sibling adapters plus a signal
   consumed in another subtree (N73). Crossed as one element, not split.
5. **N85 may be hiding a store**: the name carries "cached" but no held data set appears in its
   coverage. "If a normalised search cache is genuinely data the system holds, a `store` element is
   missing from the product model."
6. **N16 is a statement in a parent position** — the sole reason UAT and user-documentation items exist
   in the UX subtree.
