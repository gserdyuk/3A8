How wrong can an estimate be?

The honest spread of an estimate against the eventual actual, stage by stage — and three things the “cone of uncertainty” does not tell you.

“Cone of uncertainty”: Boehm 1981 → McConnell 2006log scale · actual ÷ estimate

What the cone won’t tell you

  • It does not narrow by itself. The cone is the best case (Little, 2006). Narrowing is bought with questions, prototypes, re-estimation — with work, not with time.
  • The error is asymmetric. Average overrun is +30–40% (Moløkken & Jørgensen, 2003); about 1 project in 6 is a black swan at ~+200% (Flyvbjerg & Budzier, 2011). The right tail is longer.
  • Part of the “error” isn’t error. Scope drift: you built a different project than you estimated. An estimate without its assumption list is an estimate of nobody knows what.

Operating rules

  • An early estimate is a range, not a point. A point at RFP stage is naivety — or a negotiating position.
  • P50 is not a promise: half of all projects exceed the median. By definition.
  • Make the corridor asymmetric: P90 sits farther from the center than P10.
  • An honest 80% interval must miss ~1 time in 5. An interval that never misses is uselessly wide.
  • Size the tail reserve from similar-project statistics, not “+20% for risks” on top of a WBS.
  • Keep assumptions and open questions next to the number — or every argument about a miss becomes an argument about what was estimated.
